# Observation and hypothesis

Written before any product is named, which is the test.

## Observation

A family in Hyderabad decides to buy a home. Over the next four months they will be
worked by, on a normal transaction: two or three channel partner firms who each found
them through a different ad, a sales executive at each project they enquire about, a site
executive who shows them the flat, and eventually somebody from the builder's own office.

Every one of those people keeps their own list. Usually a spreadsheet, sometimes a
notebook, often just WhatsApp.

So three things happen, every time.

The family is asked the same qualifying questions by four different people, and by the
third time they have stopped answering honestly.

Two partners discover they registered the same phone number and argue about the
commission. The argument happens on WhatsApp, the developer decides based on who shouts
louder or who they like more, and the losing firm quietly stops sending buyers.

The builder, who owns the inventory and is paying for the advertising, cannot answer the
question "how did my project sell this week" without asking a person to make him a
spreadsheet, which arrives two days later and is already wrong.

I have been on all three sides of this. I run the advisory that sits in the middle of it.

## Hypothesis

The cause is not effort and it is not software. Everyone involved is working hard and
most of them already own a CRM.

The cause is that the family has no identity. The record of that family exists in
everybody's sheet and nobody's system, so there is nothing for the work to accumulate
against.

If a family is given one permanent identity at first contact, and every subsequent touch
is written against it regardless of which firm or which surface generated the touch, then:

- the qualifying questions get asked once, because the answers are on the record
- attribution becomes a lookup with a decision log instead of an argument
- the builder reads his own inventory instead of requesting a report

## Falsifier

If, after the single record exists and is populated, partners keep disputing attribution
at the same rate, and executives keep re-asking questions that are already on the record,
then the observation is wrong. The problem was behaviour, not data, and a system will not
fix it.

This is testable now. Disputes are counted in the record. The re-asking is measurable by
sitting with five executives and timing them.

## The sixty second version

> In Hyderabad one family is worked by several partners, several project teams and the
> builder at once, and each keeps its own list. The family answers the same questions four
> times, two partners claim the same buyer and argue, and the builder has to ask someone
> for a spreadsheet to find out how his own project sold. The record of that family exists
> in everybody's sheet and nobody's system. We give the family one identity at first
> contact and write every later touch against it. Nine thousand one hundred families now
> have that record, six thousand four hundred of them lifted out of spreadsheets, and two
> hundred and thirty four partner firms work against it.
