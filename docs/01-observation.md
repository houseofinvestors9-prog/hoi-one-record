# Observation and hypothesis

Written before any product is named. That is the test.

## Observation

A family in Hyderabad decides to buy a flat. Over the next four months, on a normal
transaction, they will be worked by two or three partner firms who each found them
through a different ad, a sales executive at every project they enquire about, a site
executive who shows them the flat, and eventually somebody from the builder's own office.

Every one of them keeps a list of their own. Usually a spreadsheet, sometimes a notebook,
often just WhatsApp.

So the same three things happen on every transaction.

By the third call the family has stopped answering properly, because they have given the
same five answers to four different people.

Two firms find out they registered the same number. The argument happens on WhatsApp, the
developer decides based on who shouts louder or who he likes more, and the firm that loses
quietly stops sending buyers.

The builder is paying for the advertising and owns the flats, but to find out how the week
went he has to ask someone for a sheet. It arrives two days later and it is already wrong.

I run the advisory that sits in the middle of this. I have been on all three sides of it.

## Hypothesis

The cause is not the effort. Everyone involved is working hard and most of them already
own a CRM that they use properly.

The gap is that there is no common record of the family, so nothing adds up across firms.

Give the family one permanent ID at first contact, write every later touch against it
whichever firm produced that touch, and three things follow:

- the qualifying questions get asked once, because the answers are already on the record
- the commission claim becomes a lookup with a decision log
- the builder reads his own inventory instead of asking for a report

## Falsifier

If firms keep disputing attribution at the same rate now that the record exists, and
executives keep re-asking questions already sitting on it, the observation is wrong. The
problem was behaviour and not data, and no system fixes that.

Both halves are measurable today. Disputes are counted in the record. The re-asking can be
measured by sitting with five executives and timing them.

## The sixty second version

> In Hyderabad one family gets worked by several firms, several project teams and the
> builder at the same time, and each of them keeps a list of their own. The family answers
> the same questions four times, two firms claim the same buyer and fight about it, and the
> builder has to ask someone for a sheet to find out how his own project sold. There is no
> common record of that family anywhere. We give the family one ID at first contact and
> write every later touch against it. 9,100 families now have that record, 6,415 of them
> pulled out of spreadsheets, and 234 partner firms work against it.
