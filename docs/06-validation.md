# Validation, the part that is still open

The capstone review is specific about this. Five people outside friends and family, at
least one controlling a budget, asked **after** being told the free alternatives exist. A
baseline number in minutes or rupees. Otherwise the benefit is an adjective.

This is the plan. It is not a claim that the work is done.

## Who to ask

The advantage is that the users are not friends. They are 234 competing firms and the
builders whose flats are on the line. None of them owe us anything.

| # | Who | Why they will not just agree to be polite |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A principal at a large firm already on the desk | Runs a P&L. Has walked away from developers over commission disputes |
| 2 | A principal at a small or individual firm | Different pain. One disputed lead is a month of income |
| 3 | A builder currently giving us inventory | Controls the budget. Decides whether to hand over the next project |
| 4 | A builder **not** working with us | The real test. No relationship to protect |
| 5 | A sales head at a competing advisory running its own firm network | Will say if a spreadsheet is genuinely fine |

Three and four are the ones that count. A builder signs the cheque.

## What to ask

Order matters. Baseline before solution, and the awareness test before the verdict.

1. Walk me through the last time two of your firms claimed the same buyer. What happened,
   who decided, how long did it take?
2. In a normal month, how many times does that happen?
3. When a buyer calls back after six months, how do you find out what was already
   discussed, and how long does that take?
4. When you want to know how your project sold this week, who do you ask and when do you
   get it?
5. Asked before any verdict: all of this can sit in a shared sheet, and ChatGPT will
   summarise the sheet for you for twenty dollars a month. Knowing that, would you still
   want a system that holds the record and settles the dispute?
6. If yes, what is it worth per month, and what would have to be true before you signed?

Question five separates a real problem from a problem people have simply not solved yet.
Record the answer word for word.

## The baseline to write down

For each conversation, a number and not an impression.

- Minutes to reconstruct a returning buyer's history today
- Disputes per month, and hours spent settling one
- Days between asking for a sales report and getting it
- Rupees lost on the last commission dispute that went badly

## Inside the company, the same exercise

27 people use this every day. Ask five of them, by stopwatch and not by memory.

Time them opening a returning buyer and working out where things stand, on the system.
Then ask them to do the same from the legacy sheets in `LeadHistory`, which are still in
the database, and time that.

The second measurement is the before number. It exists, it is reproducible, and it takes
an afternoon. It is the single most valuable missing piece in this submission.
