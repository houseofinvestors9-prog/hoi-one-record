# Validation: the part that is still open

The capstone review is explicit about this. Five people outside friends and family, at
least one controlling a budget, asked **after** being told the free alternatives exist.
A baseline number in minutes or rupees. Otherwise the benefit is an adjective.

This document is the plan, not a claim that it is done.

## Who to ask

The advantage here is that the users are not friends. They are 234 competing partner
firms and the builders whose inventory is on the line. None of them owe anything.

| # | Who | Why they are not a false positive |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A partner principal from a large firm already on the desk | Runs a P&L, has walked away from developers over commission disputes |
| 2 | A partner principal from a small or individual firm | Different pain. One disputed lead is a month of income |
| 3 | A builder currently giving inventory to House of Investors | Controls the budget. Decides whether to hand over the next project |
| 4 | A builder **not** working with House of Investors | The real test. No relationship to protect |
| 5 | A sales head at a competing advisory running their own partner network | Will say if a spreadsheet is genuinely fine |

Three and four are the ones that count. A builder signs the cheque.

## What to ask

Order matters. Baseline before solution, and the awareness test before the verdict.

1. Walk me through the last time two of your partners claimed the same buyer. What
   happened, who decided, how long did it take?
2. In a normal month, how many times does that happen?
3. When a buyer calls you back after six months, how do you find out what was already
   discussed, and how long does that take?
4. When you want to know how your project sold this week, who do you ask and when do you
   get it?
5. *(the awareness test, asked before any verdict)* All of this can be kept in a shared
   sheet, and ChatGPT can summarise the sheet for you for twenty dollars a month. Knowing
   that, would you still want a system that holds the record and arbitrates the dispute?
6. If yes, what is it worth per month, and what would have to be true before you signed?

Question five is the one that separates a real problem from a problem people just have
not solved yet. Record the answer verbatim.

## The baseline to record

For each conversation, write down a number, not an impression:

- Minutes to reconstruct a returning buyer's history today
- Disputes per month, and hours spent settling one
- Days between asking for a sales report and getting it
- Rupees lost on the last commission dispute that went badly

## Inside the company, do the same

Twenty seven people use this daily. Ask five of them, by stopwatch, not memory:

- Time to open a returning buyer and know where things stand. Measure it on the system
  now. Then ask them to do the same from the legacy sheets in `LeadHistory`, which are
  still there, and time that.

That second measurement is the before number. It exists, it is reproducible, and it takes
an afternoon. It is the single most valuable missing piece in this submission.
