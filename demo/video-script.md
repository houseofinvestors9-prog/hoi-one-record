# Demo video: script and shot list

Target length **2 minutes 30 seconds**. Shorter beats complete.

One rule for the whole video: **do not show the product before the problem lands.** The
capstone review failed six of nine pitches for opening with the build.

Three things carry the retention. The cold open states the fight in ten seconds. The
first number arrives at 0:40 rather than 1:33. The close leads with what is proved and
concedes what is not, in that order.

---

## Shot 0 · Cold open · 0:00 to 0:10

Black, then the words. No voice for the first beat, then:

> Two firms. The same buyer. One commission.
>
> That fight used to happen on WhatsApp. Nobody wrote down who won.

Cut hard into shot 1. The fight is not explained here. It is paid off in shot 4 and
called back in shot 7.

## Shot 1 · 0:10 to 0:30 · the four months

> A family in Hyderabad decides to buy a flat.
>
> Over the next four months they will be worked by two or three partner firms, a sales
> executive at every project they enquire about, a site executive who shows them the
> flat, and someone from the builder's office.
>
> Every one of them keeps a list of their own.

Pause. Then:

> So by the third call the family has stopped answering properly. And the builder, who
> is paying for the ads and owns the flats, has to ask somebody for a sheet to find out
> how his own project sold this week.

## Shot 2 · 0:30 to 0:42 · the problem named

> There is no common record of that family anywhere.
>
> That is the whole problem. Not the effort, and not the software. Most of these people
> already have a CRM and use it properly. There is just nothing for the work to add up
> against.
>
> So we gave the family one.

## Shot 3 · 0:42 to 0:50 · the early proof

Two number cards, hard cut between them. This is the shot that was missing. A reviewer
deciding at the thirty second mark whether this is serious now has something to weigh.

> Nine thousand one hundred families have one today. Six thousand four hundred of them
> were rows in a spreadsheet three weeks ago.

## Shot 4 · Screen · 0:50 to 1:10 · the record

Open one family record in the Lead Engine. Scroll it slowly. Show the `client_id` field
clearly, it is the hero of the video.

> One permanent ID, issued the first time a family touches us. Every later touch gets
> written against it.
>
> The ad they clicked and which creative it was. Every call and what came of it. The site
> visit. The budget they stated and the budget they actually have. The cheque. The
> booking.
>
> And when they come back eighteen months later for the second property, it is all still
> here.

## Shot 5 · Screen · 1:10 to 1:35 · attribution, and the payoff

Show a lead marked `duplicate`, then a `contested` one with its audit log entry.

> 234 partner firms register buyers here. They compete with each other and they do not
> trust each other.
>
> First registration owns the buyer for ninety days. A second firm registering the same
> number gets marked duplicate automatically. Where both have a real claim it goes to
> arbitration, and the decision is written to a log that nobody can edit, including me.
>
> 24 duplicates caught. 3 disputes decided.

"Including me" is the strongest line in the script. Hold the audit log on screen through
it and do not cut away.

## Shot 6 · Screen · 1:35 to 1:58 · the public layer

hyderabad.properties. Search a project, open a report, show the sources and dates. Then
the review desk with something waiting for approval.

> This part is public and needs no login. It reads the records nobody reads. RERA filings
> and orders, building permits, lake boundaries, government guideline values.
>
> Claude reads the new documents. A person approves every verdict before it publishes,
> because being wrong in public about somebody's project carries legal exposure.
>
> And a buyer who checks a project here becomes a family record in the same engine. That
> closes the loop.

## Shot 7 · Title cards · 1:58 to 2:08 · the rest of the numbers

Hold each for about two seconds. The two headline numbers already ran in shot 3.

> Thirty to forty thousand contacts logged against them. Twenty seven people using it
> every working day. Seventy four bookings closed on it.

## Shot 8 · 2:08 to 2:30 · the close

Proved first. Conceded second. Never the other way round.

> Six thousand four hundred families who used to be rows in a spreadsheet now have one
> record each. Two hundred and thirty four firms that compete with each other work
> against it. And the commission fight from the start of this video is now a decision
> with a log.

Pause. Then:

> What I have not measured is the minutes this saves per family. I know the method, it
> takes an afternoon, and it is the next thing I do.

End card: **One record for one family. So nobody asks the same question twice.**

## What changed from the first version, and why

| Change | Reason |
|---|---|
| Cold open added | The old first line was a setup, not a hook. The first five seconds decide whether the rest gets watched |
| WhatsApp fight moved to 0:00 | It was the most vivid thing in the script and it sat at 0:14 |
| Numbers split, two moved to 0:42 | The old cut gave a reviewer no proof for the first 93 seconds |
| Close reversed | Leading on what is not measured puts the weakest sentence in the strongest position |
| Callback added in shot 8 | The cold open now earns its place by paying off at the end |

## Production notes

**Record the screen first.** Three captures in order: a family record scrolled slowly, a
duplicate and then a contested lead with its audit entry, and hyderabad.properties with
the review desk.

**Blur or replace every real name and phone number.** These are real families. Use a
staging record or blur in post. This is not optional.

**Backup.** The pitch template asks for a recorded demo in case the network fails on the
day. This video is that backup. Keep it under three minutes.

**Voice.** Plain, with the weight in the right places. No music under the numbers, no
adjectives. The numbers carry it or they do not.
